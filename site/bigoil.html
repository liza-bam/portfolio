Sales modernization at BP

BP’s US fuel sales across four markets, moved from offline work into one connected system on Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal.

Download the printed case (PDF)
Team
Six-person DocuSign team inside a larger cross-functional team
Platforms and Tools
Timeline
2021–2023

Problem

$2.1B in sales run four different ways, mostly offline, with duplicate data entry, manual work across several systems, no shared standards, no knowledge sharing between teams and no view of sales in progress.

My role

As UX and service designer, I redesigned the sales processes for four US markets end to end across Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal. As DocuSign delivery lead, I created a contract system built from modular blocks that scales to any number of agreement types.

Result

The program delivered one system: data is entered once and stored in Salesforce, all four markets share processes and contract templates, dashboards show sales in progress, and a Center of Excellence keeps training and reference in one place.

Before

Each market kept its own contracts on personal hard drives and ran its own steps. The same customer data went into 5 systems, decisions drew on 50 different sources, and 100+ hours a month went to searching, filing and waiting.

1. Discovery across four markets

  • UX and service designer

Problem

Each market sold its own way, and people in different markets didn’t know each other.

Challenge

Aligning stakeholders across markets who had never worked together.

What I did

Found champions in each market and ran workshops with them over several weeks. Mapped the as-is processes, designed the to-be processes and walked them through with stakeholders to agree on where the project would go.

See the delivery plan+

The to-be processes now had to work on three legacy platforms.

2. A smooth experience across three platforms

  • UX and service designer

Problem

Every sales process crossed Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal.

Challenge

The platforms were large and legacy, so a modern experience had to fit what they could actually do. This was not a greenfield design.

What I did

Worked through every process diagram with the architects and leads of the Salesforce, Automation and DocuSign teams, and designed how users move between the platforms. The leads contributed; I owned and presented the deliverables. A large part of my work was shaping the team and stakeholders around a design-first approach.

Under the processes sat about 60,000 pages of contracts, different in every market.

3. A modular contract system

  • UX and service designer
  • DocuSign delivery lead

Problem

Each market kept its own contracts, with its own documents and steps.

Challenge

Most of the developers were junior, so I trained them alongside delivery.

What I did

Collected and processed all the contracts and designed one template structure for all four markets, built from reusable modules. Designed the move from Salesforce to DocuSign and the DocuSign flows: from a button in Salesforce, DocuSign assembles the right contract package for the market, loads the signers and runs signing in up to five stages, each visible in Salesforce. Nothing is uploaded, everything is stored in DocuSign, with reports. Led the DocuSign team of an architect and developers through daily stand-ups.

Once the first market was live and tested, people had to learn the new way of working.

4. Launch

  • UX and service designer
  • DocuSign delivery lead

Problem

Sales managers and operators had to switch to new processes full of sub-processes and edge cases.

Challenge

The testers worked at different times and had very different skills, so I organized testing asynchronously and wrote plans that worked for each of them.

What I did

Recorded a video and a cheat sheet for every flow. Came up with the Center of Excellence: built the first process as the example and led 3 business analysts to cover the rest. Wrote test plans for 3 testers, organized them in SharePoint, with results logged in Jira. Ran demos for the full 50–70 person team that doubled as reviews for bugs and edge cases, followed by regression.

Lessons learned

Agree on success before the build

The Salesforce and DocuSign teams brought different skills and different views of success. Before any build started, both needed the same picture of the process, who owns what, and what done looks like.

Unify documents before development

Agile delivery breaks down when the documents behind a process keep changing. Turning 60,000 pages of contracts into one template structure first gave the team a stable base to build the flows on.

Finish one market before starting the next

Building similar processes in parallel led to duplicated work and different ways of handling data. Launching and testing one market first, then reusing it for the next, was faster and more consistent.

Write handoffs for teams across time zones

With teams spread across nine time zones, work could not depend on everyone being online at once. Written handoffs kept every team clear on what was decided, where the work stood and what came next.
