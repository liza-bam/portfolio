Looped In student portal

Rewarding everyday campus activities in an app each university could make its own.

The portal linked six everyday student journeys with rewards chosen by schools and local businesses.

WireflowsWebsite prototype
Team

Timeline
2015

Problem

Every year more than 20 million full-time students enroll in US colleges, and two out of three do not graduate. LoopedIn, a 2015 startup, wanted to support retention through campus rewards, with faculty, students, parents and local businesses all taking part.

My role

As UX/UI designer and design manager, I mapped the stakeholders and rewards, drew the main journeys and the acceptance-testing flow, and designed the UI with a skin system for each university.

Result

The app launched as the first of its kind on the market. 150 screens were designed and built in one month, and four universities were onboarded, each with its own skin.

1. Stakeholders and rewards

  • UX/UI designer

Problem

Four groups had a part in one student’s habits: parents, faculty, students and local vendors.

Challenge

What the school wanted and what students wanted were different things, and both had to be served by one app.

What I did

Mapped what the school wants against what students want, and put parents, faculty, students and local vendors in the same loop, with rewards from the school and from local businesses.

The six everyday jobs then had to become one app.

2. Six journeys in one app

  • UX/UI designer
  • Design manager

Problem

One app had to do six jobs a student does every day: pay, send money, find events, get rewards, buy books and find places to eat.

Challenge

Several teams built different features, and the features had to work as one app.

What I did

Drew the main journeys so each team could see where its features met, and a user flow for acceptance testing.

See the user flow for acceptance testing+

Rewards then had to run through all six journeys.

3. Rewards inside every feature

  • UX/UI designer

Problem

Rewards had to reach students where they were already looking.

Challenge

The school had to be able to promote exactly the events it needs students to attend.

What I did

Made rewards part of every feature instead of a tab of their own. The calendar marks rewarded events, the campus store tells you what earns points, and the map shows where your free drink is waiting.

Each school then had to see its own brand in the app.

4. A skin system for each college

  • UX/UI designer
  • Design manager

Problem

The app had to look like each school that bought it, not like a vendor.

Challenge

90% of US colleges use red, blue, gold and white, so colors alone could not tell one school from another.

What I did

Designed a skin system: each college chooses its widgets, menu, colors and main graphics. We made a custom icon font for the shared actions and the school-specific ones, and color presets so a school picks a set instead of designing one.

See the custom icon font+

Result

The app launched as the first of its kind on the market. Deliverables: the student portal with six journeys and rewards throughout, a shared interface and icon system, and university-specific themes.
