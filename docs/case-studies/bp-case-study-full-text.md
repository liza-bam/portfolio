# BP case study — full text (for the online version)

Agreed with Sandy on November 25, 2026. The printed portfolio uses a shorter version; this full text is kept for the online case study at https://sandy.tayts.com/bigoil.html.

## Header

**Title:** Sales modernization at BP

**Subtitle:** BP's US fuel sales across four markets, moved from offline work into one connected system on Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal.

**Team:** Six-person DocuSign team inside a larger cross-functional team
**Timeline:** 2021–2023

## TL;DR

**Problem:** $2.1B in sales run four different ways, mostly offline, with duplicate data entry, manual work across several systems, no shared standards, no knowledge sharing between teams and no view of sales in progress.

**My role:** As **UX and service designer**, I redesigned the sales processes for four US markets end to end across Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal. As **DocuSign delivery lead**, I created a contract system built from modular blocks that scales to any number of agreement types.

**Result:** The program delivered one system: data is entered once and stored in Salesforce, all four markets share processes and contract templates, dashboards show sales in progress, and a Center of Excellence keeps training and reference in one place.
- 8 days per deal
- 0.7 hours of user time per process
- 98% of agreements online
- 8 manual processes

## Before

Each market kept its own contracts on personal hard drives and ran its own steps. The same customer data went into 5 systems, decisions drew on 50 different sources, and 100+ hours a month went to searching, filing and waiting.

- 15.5 days per deal
- 1.9 hours of user time per process
- 30% of agreements online
- 35 manual processes

## 1. Discovery across four markets

**Problem:** Each market sold its own way, and people in different markets didn't know each other.

**What I did:** Found champions in each market and ran workshops with them over several weeks. Mapped the as-is processes, designed the to-be processes and walked them through with stakeholders to agree on where the project would go.

**Challenge:** Aligning stakeholders across markets who had never worked together.

Visual: East Coast Transfer blueprint, to be. Each of the four markets got its own.

*The to-be processes now had to work on three legacy platforms.*

## 2. A smooth experience across three platforms

**Problem:** Every sales process crossed Salesforce, DocuSign and the BP portal.

**What I did:** Worked through every process diagram with the architects and leads of the Salesforce, Automation and DocuSign teams, and designed how users move between the platforms. The leads contributed; I owned and presented the deliverables. A large part of my work was shaping the team and stakeholders around a design-first approach.

**Challenge:** The platforms were large and legacy, so a modern experience had to fit what they could actually do. This was not a greenfield design.

Visual: High level ecosystem for the sales process.

*Under the processes sat about 60,000 pages of contracts, different in every market.*

## 3. A modular contract system

**Problem:** Each market kept its own contracts, with its own documents and steps.

**What I did:** Collected and processed all the contracts and designed one template structure for all four markets, built from reusable modules. Designed the move from Salesforce to DocuSign and the DocuSign flows: from a button in Salesforce, DocuSign assembles the right contract package for the market, loads the signers and runs signing in up to five stages, each visible in Salesforce. Nothing is uploaded, everything is stored in DocuSign, with reports. Led the DocuSign team of an architect and developers through daily stand-ups.

**Challenge:** Most of the developers were junior, so I trained them alongside delivery.

Visual: DocuSign design and templates alignment map: contract flow, documents and actors by type of sale.

*Once the first market was live and tested, people had to learn the new way of working.*

## 4. Launch

**Problem:** Sales managers and operators had to switch to new processes full of sub-processes and edge cases.

**What I did:** Recorded a video and a cheat sheet for every flow. Came up with the Center of Excellence: built the first process as the example and led 3 business analysts to cover the rest. Wrote test plans for 3 testers, organized them in SharePoint, with results logged in Jira. Ran demos for the full 50–70 person team that doubled as reviews for bugs and edge cases, followed by regression.

**Challenge:** The testers worked at different times and had very different skills, so I organized testing asynchronously and wrote plans that worked for each of them.

Visual: Center of Excellence home.

## Result

All four markets now work in Salesforce and DocuSign, with shared processes and contract templates.

- 8 days per deal, from 15.5
- 0.7 hours of user time per process, from 1.9
- 98% of agreements online, from 30%
- 8 manual processes, from 35
- <1,000 pages of contracts, from 60,000
- 83% positive experience, from 55%
